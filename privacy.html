Privacy Policy & Structural Commitments

Every commitment you record is yours. This page explains—in plain language and in binding operational detail—what DYON does with data, what it refuses to do, and how its architecture enforces those refusals.

0. Who we are, and what this policy covers

DYON (دَين) is a personal commitments ledger. It exists so that a person can turn every obligation they carry—money, objects, services, promises, favours, rights owed, and devotional duties—from a hidden weight into a written, honoured, and released record.

This policy applies to the DYON web application at dyon.store and to any associated client that connects to the same service. It is written to satisfy the disclosure requirements of the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the California Consumer Privacy Act as amended by the CPRA, and equivalent regimes in other jurisdictions in which DYON operates.

One sentence, if you read nothing else: DYON does not sell, rent, trade, or share your commitment data with any third party for any commercial purpose—ever—and its architecture is designed so that the operator cannot read the content of your records even if compelled.

DYON is not a lender, a debt collector, a credit bureau, an interest engine, a shaming platform, a fatwa authority, or a replacement for therapy or legal counsel. Those exclusions are not marketing disclaimers; they are structural red lines that are enforced in the application's code and are enumerated in §2 below.

2. Structural red lines—enforced in code, not merely stated

DYON's “About” page already declares a set of red lines. This section restates them as binding privacy commitments. They are not aspirational; they are architectural constraints, and a breach of any of them is a breach of this policy and of the license under which DYON operates.

ID Red line Privacy consequence
RL-01 No riba or interest computation, ever. DYON will never compute, display, or store an interest accrual on your commitments.
RL-02 No selling or sharing of user data. No data broker, no advertiser, no “trusted partner” receives your records. There is no consent toggle that enables this, because the capability does not exist.
RL-03 No public shaming, no defaulter lists. No public profile, no searchable debtor index, no social feed of unpaid obligations.
RL-04 No credit score shared with third parties. DYON does not generate a credit score and will not disclose your record to any credit reference agency, lender, or insurer.
RL-05 No dark patterns or manufactured urgency. No countdown timers designed to coerce, no shame-based push notifications, no “your reputation will suffer” messaging.
RL-06 No lending or loan brokering. DYON does not originate credit, does not match borrowers to lenders, and does not facilitate interest-bearing arrangements.
RL-07 No algorithmic decisions against users. Hardship is self-declared. No model infers your creditworthiness, your reliability, or your “risk level” from your behaviour.
RL-08 Minimal data for non-users. If someone appears as a counterparty but never creates an account, DYON retains only the minimum needed to display the record to you, and deletes it on request.

3. How your data is used—and what it is never used for

DYON uses your data for four purposes and no others:

  1. To operate the ledger. Display your commitments, compute your gross and net position, surface clearing opportunities, send the reminders you configure, and generate exports.
  2. To protect the service. Detect abuse, prevent unauthorised access, and maintain availability.
  3. To meet legal obligations. Respond to valid legal process, security incidents, and statutory notices—subject to the compelled-disclosure safeguards in §6.
  4. To measure impact, only with your separate consent. The research programme described in §7 is opt-in, de-identified, and governed by an independent scientific board.
What DYON never does with your data: it does not train advertising models on it, does not sell it, does not use it to rank you against other users, does not disclose it to a counterparty without your explicit per-message action, and does not use your hardship disclosures to market a financial product to you.

4. Sharing and disclosure

4.1 No commercial sharing

DYON does not sell, rent, trade, or otherwise make your data available to any third party for monetary or other valuable consideration. This is RL-02 and it is absolute. There is no “Do Not Sell” toggle because there is no sale to opt out of—the category of transaction does not exist in DYON's architecture.

4.2 Limited operational processors

DYON uses a small number of infrastructure providers (hosting, encrypted backup, payment processing, and transactional email). Each is bound by a data-processing agreement that prohibits secondary use, requires deletion on termination, and subjects the processor to the same compelled-disclosure safeguards described in §6. The current list of processors, their locations, and the categories of data they touch is published at dyon.store/processors and updated whenever the list changes.

4.3 User-initiated disclosure

The only circumstance in which a counterparty sees information about your commitment is when you take an explicit action: sending a message template, inviting them to co-sign a record, or generating a shareable export. DYON does not notify a counterparty automatically, does not send them a summary of “what Ahmed owes you,” and does not make your ledger visible to them by default.

4.4 Legal process and compelled disclosure

DYON may disclose data where required by a valid court order, subpoena, or equivalent legal process. However, the zero-knowledge architecture described in §6 means that in the ordinary case the operator holds only ciphertext and cannot produce plaintext. Where DYON receives a demand it believes is overbroad or unlawful, it will:

  • seek to narrow the demand;
  • publish the number and general nature of demands received in its annual transparency report; and
  • notify the affected user before disclosure where legally permitted and where doing so would not create a risk of harm.

4.5 Business transfers

If DYON is acquired, merged, or its assets are transferred, commitment records remain encrypted with keys the acquiring entity does not possess. Personal data may not be transferred to a holder whose intended use falls outside the purpose for which it was collected without fresh consent meeting the standard in §5.3 or destruction of the data.

4.6 No special-category sharing

DYON does not intentionally collect special-category data (health, religion, sexual orientation, political opinion). If you include such information in a free-text commitment description, it remains on your device under the same encryption as the rest of your record, and it is never extracted, aggregated, or shared.

5.1 The right to erasure is operational, not theoretical

RL-09 requires one-click full deletion. When you exercise it:

  • your commitment records are deleted from your device and from every encrypted backup within 30 days;
  • your account identifier is destroyed;
  • any research data you contributed is unlinked from your identity (aggregate results that cannot be reversed are retained, as permitted by GDPR Art. 17(3)(d)); and
  • you receive a deletion receipt confirming what was removed and when.

There is no “soft delete” that keeps your data for “analytics,” and no retention period that quietly extends beyond what is stated here.

6. Security, encryption, and the compelled-disclosure safeguard

6.1 Zero-knowledge architecture

Commitment records are encrypted on your device with keys derived from your credentials. The operator stores ciphertext and does not possess the decryption keys. This means that in the ordinary course of operation, the operator cannot read your records, cannot search them, and cannot produce them in readable form in response to a request—because it does not have them.

6.2 What this does and does not guarantee

  • It does guarantee that a database breach exposes ciphertext, not your commitments; and that a legal demand for content is answerable only with material the operator actually holds.
  • It does not guarantee that no metadata exists (for example, that an account exists and when it was last active), and it does not protect you if your own device or credentials are compromised.
  • It does not make DYON immune to a lawful order directed at you personally—for example, an order requiring you to produce your own records. DYON's architecture protects against compelled disclosure by the operator; it is not a shield against a court order addressed to you.

6.3 Additional safeguards

  • Transport encryption (TLS) for all network communication.
  • Encryption at rest for backups, with keys held separately from the ciphertext.
  • Access to production systems limited to a small number of named individuals, logged, and reviewed.
  • Independent security review before any material change to the encryption model.
  • A published security.txt and a coordinated-vulnerability-disclosure policy.
The compelled-disclosure safeguard in one sentence: because DYON does not hold the keys, a demand for the content of your ledger cannot be satisfied by the operator even under compulsion—the operator can only disclose what it actually possesses, which is ciphertext and limited metadata. This is a technical fact, not a promise of non-cooperation.

6.4 Breach notification

If a breach occurs that is likely to result in a risk to your rights and freedoms, DYON will notify affected users without undue delay and, where required, the relevant supervisory authority within 72 hours of becoming aware. Because records are encrypted, the notification will state accurately what categories of data were exposed and whether plaintext was at risk.

7. Optional research participation

DYON runs a separate, opt-in longitudinal study to measure whether the ledger actually reduces commitment-related anxiety and improves fulfilment. Participation is not a condition of using DYON, and declining has no effect on any feature.

7.1 What participation involves

  • Short validated questionnaires (for example, GAD-2 and PHQ-2 for anxiety and mood, administered at baseline and then at intervals).
  • Aggregate indicators such as the number of commitments closed and the proportion closed by clearing rather than by payment.
  • Optional qualitative interviews, separately consented.

7.2 What is never collected in research

  • Your name, email, or account identifier in linked form.
  • The content of your commitment descriptions.
  • Counterparty identities.
  • Any data that could re-identify you from a small group.

7.3 Governance

Research is governed by an independent scientific board with a published membership, a pre-registered protocol, and a commitment to publish negative and inconclusive results alongside positive ones. The board's findings are published unedited. You may withdraw from the study at any time without affecting your use of DYON, and withdrawal triggers unlinking of your contributed data.

9. Children and minors

DYON is not directed at persons under 18. It does not knowingly collect personal information from a minor. If DYON learns that a minor has created an account, the account and its records will be deleted without penalty to the minor.

Where a Deployment is directed at or knowingly reaches persons under 18, the default configuration must be the most protective available: no behavioural profiling, no engagement-optimised ranking, no unsolicited contact from strangers, and data retention limited to what the function requires. A protective default may not be weakened by consent obtained from the minor.

10. International transfers

DYON is designed to minimise cross-border data movement. Commitment records are stored on your device and in encrypted backups whose location is disclosed in the processor list at dyon.store/processors. Where personal data is transferred from the EEA, the UK, or Switzerland to a country without an adequacy decision, DYON relies on Standard Contractual Clauses or an equivalent lawful transfer mechanism, supplemented by a transfer impact assessment.

Because the operator does not hold plaintext commitment records, the practical risk of a transfer is limited to metadata and ciphertext. That fact does not remove the legal obligation to provide a lawful transfer mechanism, and DYON provides one.

11. Cookies and local storage

DYON does not use advertising cookies, tracking pixels, or third-party analytics that profile you across sites. It uses:

  • Strictly necessary storage (session authentication, security tokens, and your local ledger cache) that does not require consent;
  • Preference storage (language, theme, and display settings) that is stored locally and is not transmitted; and
  • No marketing or analytics cookies unless you explicitly opt in to a privacy-preserving, aggregate measurement mechanism that does not use cookies.

You can clear local storage through your browser at any time; doing so will remove locally cached records that have not been backed up.
